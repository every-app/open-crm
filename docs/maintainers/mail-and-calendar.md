# Mail and calendar

Each member connects their own Gmail and Google Calendar, or their Outlook mail, from Settings → Connections. The CRM reads them (never sends, changes or drafts anything) and files their work conversations and meetings on the organization's companies and contacts, adding the records it should. The code is `src/server/features/mailbox`.

## What the operator sets up

The gateway holds the two OAuth clients (`google-workspace`, `microsoft-365` in `everyapp.config.ts` `capabilities`, with the provider's own scope names) and each member's connection; the CRM holds no client and no token. Until an operator adds a client, its page under Settings → Connections says what to set, in the gateway's words (owners and admins also see the command).

```bash
pnpm everyapp secret set GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_CLIENT_ID GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_CLIENT_SECRET
pnpm everyapp secret set MICROSOFT_365_CLIENT_ID MICROSOFT_365_CLIENT_SECRET
```

Register the gateway's redirect URI on each client: `https://<gateway host>/connect/google-workspace/callback` (scopes `gmail.readonly` and `calendar.readonly`) and `https://<gateway host>/connect/microsoft-365/callback` (Graph `User.Read` and `Mail.Read`, for accounts in any organization and personal accounts).

Gmail's read scope is restricted. An **Internal** consent screen (mailboxes in the operator's own Google Workspace) needs no review; an **External** one needs Google's verification and a yearly security assessment before anyone else's mailbox can connect. A member may untick Gmail or Calendar on Google's consent screen: the CRM syncs only the sources it can read.

In local dev, put an access token you obtained yourself in `.dev.vars` as `GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_ACCESS_TOKEN` or `MICROSOFT_365_ACCESS_TOKEN`: the SDK puts it on the calls in place of the gateway's connection.

## How capture works

- **Connect.** Connect sends the member to the gateway's connect page (`connectUrl`), which runs the provider's consent and comes back to `/integrations/<provider>/connected` under the member's signed-in session. A refused or cancelled consent ends on the gateway's page. Coming back, the CRM reads which mailbox the connection reaches (Gmail profile and Calendar's primary calendar, or Graph `/me`): a source Google answers 403 for wasn't granted. It refuses the mailbox with `email_doesn't_match` unless it is the member's gateway email (case aside, as the CRM's Better Auth account linking refused another address), stores the address in `mailbox_connection` and creates a `mailbox_sync` row per granted source.
- **Calls** go through `integrationFetch` with `for: { background: true, organizationId, userId }`: the mailbox owner's own connection, whoever started the check. The gateway refreshes and puts the token on; the CRM never sees it. A call the gateway answers 409 `integration_not_connected` (disconnected under Connected accounts, or the member removed) marks the connection and its sources NEEDS_RECONNECT, and the page offers Reconnect. The page also asks for a reconnect when the gateway's status reports the member's connection gone.
- **Sync** runs from the minute cron for every organization: one indexed query (`mailbox_sync_next_idx`) for the rows due, round-robin by organization and longest-waiting first, each claimed with a lease (a conditional update) and checked inside `forOrganization`. Each mailbox is checked every five minutes. A tick shares a budget of provider calls and wall time across its checks; each check stops where it got to (Gmail at a history record, Outlook at a received time, Calendar at a page) and goes to the back of the line. A message or meeting that can't be stored is logged by its id and passed over once a later one is stored; until then the check stops before it. A failure is recorded on the source and retried at its next turn; a rate limit waits as long as the provider asked. "Check now" runs the member's sources at once under the same lease; an AI client's (`check_mailbox_now`) makes them due for the next sweep instead, since a check can outlast a tool call.
- **Forward only.** Gmail's first check records the mailbox's historyId; Outlook's records the time; neither imports the past. Calendar's first check reads now to 180 days ahead, then only changes (sync token). An expired cursor starts again from now.
- **Filing** (`CaptureService`, `MatchService`): a thread is keyed by the RFC id of its first message, so the same conversation in two members' mailboxes is one thread; a message is stored once per organization. Matching goes contact by address, then the company of the people's dominant work domain, then, only when the source may add records (calendar by default; Gmail and Outlook when the member turns it on, and only once the member has written in the thread; never for a meeting they declined), a new company and contact with `source` EMAIL or CALENDAR and an ENRICHMENT activity. Our members' addresses and domains, suppressed contacts and domains, machine and automated addresses never become people, and free-mail domains never become companies. "Our" domains (`MatchService.internalIdentity`: the members' and connected mailboxes' work domains and the workspace's website) are also what website forms skip.
- **Suppress a domain** (the `suppress_domain` MCP tool, as the CRM had no screen for it): adds the normalized domain to `suppressed_domain` with an optional reason; our own domains are refused. `ConnectionService.suppressDomain` can also delete the threads and meetings filed on the organization's companies at that domain and recompute last activity (the CRM's `purge`), but the tool never does: a tool that deletes is destructive, which the gateway hides from every client.
- **Delete synced data** removes what one member's mailbox brought in, rebuilds threads other members' mail still holds, and recomputes last activity. **Disconnect** stops capture and forgets the mailbox; the connection itself is the gateway's, removed under Connected accounts on Every App.
- **Members who left.** Owners and admins see every member's connection under "Members' mailboxes" in Settings → Connections, and can delete what one synced or disconnect it, as the member would ([every-app.md](every-app.md), "Removing a member"). The CRM records when it last saw each member, at most once a day; the daily cron sets a member's sources PAUSED (no `next_sync_at`, so nothing schedules them) once it hasn't seen them for 30 days, and seeing them again makes the sources due at once, from their cursors (an expired one starts again from now, as ever).

## Known limits

- Microsoft 365 organizations that let only admins consent (Microsoft's default for `Mail.Read`) stop the member on Microsoft's approval page; the gateway's flow has no admin-consent link yet, so an admin grants consent for the gateway's client in Microsoft Entra.

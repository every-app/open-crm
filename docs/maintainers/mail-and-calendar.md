# Mail and calendar

Each member connects their own Gmail and Google Calendar, or their Outlook mail, from Settings → Connections. The CRM reads them (never sends, changes or drafts anything) and files their work conversations and meetings on the organization's companies and contacts, adding the records it should. The code is `src/server/features/mailbox`.

## What the operator sets up

The gateway holds the two OAuth clients (`google-workspace`, `microsoft-365` in `everyapp.config.ts` `capabilities`); the CRM holds none. Until an operator sets one, its page under Settings → Connections says it isn't configured (owners and admins of a fork see the command and the redirect URI).

```bash
pnpm everyapp secret set GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_CLIENT_ID GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_CLIENT_SECRET --for gateway
pnpm everyapp secret set MICROSOFT_365_CLIENT_ID MICROSOFT_365_CLIENT_SECRET --for gateway
```

Register these redirect URIs on each client, on the CRM's own host:

- Google: `https://<crm host>/integrations/google/callback`, scopes `gmail.readonly` and `calendar.readonly`.
- Microsoft: `https://<crm host>/integrations/microsoft/callback`, scopes `offline_access User.Read Mail.Read`, registered for accounts in any organization and personal accounts (the gateway completes the `common` endpoint only).

Microsoft 365 organizations that let only admins consent (Microsoft's default for `Mail.Read`, and for unverified publishers) send members to an approval page they come back from as `access_denied`; their page then links Microsoft's admin consent for their address's tenant (`adminConsent.ts`).

Gmail's read scope is restricted. An **Internal** consent screen (mailboxes in the operator's own Google Workspace) needs no review; an **External** one needs Google's verification and a yearly security assessment before anyone else's mailbox can connect. A member may untick Gmail or Calendar on Google's consent screen: the CRM keeps what was granted and syncs only those sources.

In local dev, put a client of your own in `.dev.vars` under the same names; the SDK calls the token endpoint directly with it.

## How capture works

- **Connect.** `startConnect` builds the authorize URL from the gateway's public `clientId`, with a single-use `state` (an `oauth_state` row bound to the member and organization, ten minutes) and a PKCE S256 challenge whose verifier stays in that row. The callback runs under the member's signed-in session, consumes the state with a conditional update (a replayed, expired or someone else's state is refused), exchanges the code through `integrationFetch` (the gateway adds its client), learns the mailbox's address (Gmail profile, Calendar's primary calendar, or Graph `/me`), refuses it with `email_doesn't_match` unless it is the member's gateway email (case aside, as the CRM's Better Auth account linking refused another address), stores the member's tokens in `mailbox_connection` and creates a `mailbox_sync` row per granted source.
- **Tokens** are kept in the CRM's database, encrypted with AES-GCM under the CRM's `TOKEN_ENCRYPTION_KEY` secret (`src/server/lib/tokenCrypto.ts`), as OpenSEO encrypts its Google grants; a row from before that reads as it is and is encrypted on its next write. Without the key no connect starts. They never reach the client, logs, errors, the agent or MCP tools. Refreshes go through the gateway, background ones naming the organization; the newest refresh token always wins (Microsoft rotates them), and `invalid_grant` marks the connection and its sources NEEDS_RECONNECT.
- **Sync** runs from the minute cron for every organization: one indexed query (`mailbox_sync_next_idx`) for the rows due, round-robin by organization and longest-waiting first, each claimed with a lease (a conditional update) and checked inside `forOrganization`. Each mailbox is checked every five minutes. A tick shares a budget of provider calls and wall time across its checks; each check stops where it got to (Gmail at a history record, Outlook at a received time, Calendar at a page) and goes to the back of the line. A message or meeting that can't be stored is logged by its id and passed over once a later one is stored; until then the check stops before it. A failure is recorded on the source and retried at its next turn; a rate limit waits as long as the provider asked. "Check now" runs the member's sources at once under the same lease; an AI client's (`check_mailbox_now`) makes them due for the next sweep instead, since a check can outlast a tool call.
- **Forward only.** Gmail's first check records the mailbox's historyId; Outlook's records the time; neither imports the past. Calendar's first check reads now to 180 days ahead, then only changes (sync token). An expired cursor starts again from now.
- **Filing** (`CaptureService`, `MatchService`): a thread is keyed by the RFC id of its first message, so the same conversation in two members' mailboxes is one thread; a message is stored once per organization. Matching goes contact by address, then the company of the people's dominant work domain, then, only when the source may add records (calendar by default; Gmail and Outlook when the member turns it on, and only once the member has written in the thread; never for a meeting they declined), a new company and contact with `source` EMAIL or CALENDAR and an ENRICHMENT activity. Our members' addresses and domains, suppressed contacts and domains, machine and automated addresses never become people, and free-mail domains never become companies. "Our" domains (`MatchService.internalIdentity`: the members' and connected mailboxes' work domains and the workspace's website) are also what website forms skip. A contact with no name queues the agent's `identify`; a meeting within a week queues `meeting-prep` for each attendee the agent has no brief on.
- **Suppress a domain** (the `suppress_domain` MCP tool, as the CRM had no screen for it): adds the normalized domain to `suppressed_domain` with an optional reason; our own domains are refused. `ConnectionService.suppressDomain` can also delete the threads and meetings filed on the organization's companies at that domain and recompute last activity (the CRM's `purge`), but the tool never does: a tool that deletes is destructive, which the gateway hides from every client.
- **Delete synced data** removes what one member's mailbox brought in, rebuilds threads other members' mail still holds, and recomputes last activity. **Disconnect** withdraws Google's grant at Google first, unless another member's connection uses that Google account (a shared inbox): withdrawing any token ends the account's whole grant to the gateway's client, so then it only forgets this member's tokens. The check is whether such a connection exists, and reads nothing of it. Microsoft has no revocation endpoint, so disconnecting only forgets the tokens, and the page sends the member to their Microsoft account to withdraw the consent itself.
- **Members who left.** Owners and admins see every member's connection under "Members' mailboxes" in Settings → Connections, and can delete what one synced or disconnect it, as the member would ([every-app.md](every-app.md), "Removing a member"). The CRM records when it last saw each member, at most once a day; the daily cron sets a member's sources PAUSED (no `next_sync_at`, so nothing schedules them) once it hasn't seen them for 30 days, and seeing them again makes the sources due at once, from their cursors (an expired one starts again from now, as ever).
- **Team agents** read the captured threads and meetings filed on their records only when their deployed version carries the Email or Calendar grant, which the builder offers once some member has connected a mailbox or calendar ([agent.md](agent.md), "Team agents"). They read the rows, never a mailbox or a token.

## Known limits

- A member the gateway removes keeps syncing until an owner or admin disconnects their mailbox, or for 30 days after the CRM last saw them: the gateway never says whom it removed.
